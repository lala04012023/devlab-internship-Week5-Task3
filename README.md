# A/B Testing Analysis – Landing Page Experiment

## Project Overview

This project analyzes the results of an A/B test conducted to compare two different landing page versions.

Users were randomly divided into two groups:

* **Control group** – users who saw the existing landing page
* **Treatment group** – users who saw the new landing page

The main goal of the analysis is to determine whether the new landing page improves the user conversion rate compared with the existing version.

The analysis covers data cleaning, group validation, conversion rate calculation, time-based analysis, visualizations, and business-oriented conclusions.

---

## Dataset
https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/zhangluyuan/ab-testing

The dataset contains **294,478 rows and 5 columns**.

The main variables used in the analysis include:

* `user_id` – unique identifier of each user
* `timestamp` / `date` – time when the user participated in the experiment
* `group` – experimental group (`control` or `treatment`)
* `landing_page` – landing page shown to the user
* `converted` – indicates whether the user completed the desired conversion

The Control and Treatment groups contain approximately **147K users each**, meaning that the experiment groups are well balanced.

---

## Data Cleaning

Before calculating the final conversion rates, the consistency between the experimental group and the landing page was checked.

Some users were assigned to one group but received a landing page belonging to the other group. These observations were considered **misassigned users**.

A total of **3,894 misassigned rows** were identified and removed from the analysis.

This step was important because including incorrectly assigned users could distort the comparison between the Control and Treatment groups.

---

## Conversion Rate Analysis

Conversion rate was calculated as the proportion of users who converted within each experimental group.

The results were:

| Group     | Conversion Rate |
| --------- | --------------: |
| Control   |          12.04% |
| Treatment |          11.88% |

The difference between the two groups is:

**-0.16 percentage points**

This means that the Treatment group achieved a slightly lower conversion rate than the Control group.

Therefore, based on the observed conversion rates, the new landing page does not show an improvement over the existing page.

---

## Visualizations

Several visualizations were created to understand the experiment results from different perspectives.

### 1. Conversion Rate by Group

A bar chart compares the overall conversion rates of the Control and Treatment groups.

This provides a simple overview of whether the new landing page performs better than the existing version.

### 2. Daily Conversion Rate

A line chart shows the conversion rate for both groups across different dates.

This visualization helps identify whether the experiment results change significantly over time.

The daily conversion rates remain relatively stable, suggesting that there is **no obvious temporal bias** affecting the comparison.

### 3. Funnel Chart

A funnel-style visualization compares:

**Users → Converted Users**

for both Control and Treatment groups.

This makes it easier to understand how many users entered each group and how many eventually converted.

### 4. Conversion Rate by Hour

Conversion rates were also segmented by hour of the day.

This analysis was used to investigate whether the time of day affects conversion performance and whether the Treatment group behaves differently during specific hours.

---

## Bonus Analysis

### Conversion Rate by Hour of Day

The conversion rate was calculated separately for each hour and experimental group.

The purpose was to determine whether user behavior changes depending on the time of day.

This analysis provides an additional check for possible time-related patterns that may not be visible in the overall conversion rates.

### Additional Conversions per 1M Users

The difference between Treatment and Control conversion rates was also used to estimate the expected impact at a larger scale.

The calculation follows the logic:

**(Treatment Conversion Rate − Control Conversion Rate) × 1,000,000**

A positive result would indicate additional conversions generated by the Treatment version.

A negative result would indicate that the Treatment version would generate fewer conversions than the Control version.

This metric helps translate a small percentage difference into a more practical business impact.

---

## Key Findings

* The dataset contains **294,478 rows and 5 columns**.
* The Control and Treatment groups are approximately equal in size and therefore well balanced.
* **3,894 misassigned rows** were identified and removed before the main analysis.
* The Control group has a **12.04% conversion rate**.
* The Treatment group has an **11.88% conversion rate**.
* The Treatment conversion rate is **0.16 percentage points lower** than the Control conversion rate.
* Daily conversion rates remain relatively stable, indicating **no obvious temporal bias**.
* Hourly segmentation provides an additional perspective on whether conversion performance varies throughout the day.
* The new landing page does not demonstrate better observed conversion performance than the existing page.

---

## Business Insight

The primary business takeaway is that the new landing page should **not be considered an improvement based solely on the observed conversion rates**.

Although the difference is relatively small, the Treatment group performs slightly worse than the Control group. Therefore, replacing the existing landing page with the new design does not appear justified from the current experiment results.

Before making a final product decision, statistical significance testing and confidence intervals should also be considered. A small observed difference may simply be caused by random variation rather than a real difference between the two designs.

## Methodology

The analysis followed these main steps:

1. Loaded the A/B testing dataset.
2. Inspected the dataset structure and group sizes.
3. Checked the consistency between `group` and `landing_page`.
4. Identified and removed misassigned observations.
5. Calculated overall conversion rates for Control and Treatment.
6. Compared the conversion rates between the two groups.
7. Analyzed conversion rates over time.
8. Created visualizations for group comparison and conversion trends.
9. Calculated conversion rates by hour of the day.
10. Estimated the potential conversion impact per 1 million users.
11. Summarized the findings from a business perspective.

---

## Conclusion

The A/B test indicates that the **existing landing page currently performs slightly better than the new landing page** in terms of observed conversion rate.

The Control group achieved a 12.04% conversion rate, compared with 11.88% for the Treatment group. The resulting difference of -0.16 percentage points suggests that the new design does not provide a conversion advantage.

The time-based analysis also shows relatively stable conversion behavior across days, reducing concerns about obvious temporal bias.

Overall, the current evidence does **not support adopting the new landing page as a better-performing design**. A statistical significance test would be the appropriate next step before making a final business decision.
